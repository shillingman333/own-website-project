## Alias page — explaining my alias

Catalyst comes from the fact that I want to be the catalyst in terms of "be the change". And so it feels like a good alias to have. It represents who I am.

### Who I am

I'm someone who believes that empathy unlocks potential — in the people I work with, and in myself. I care deeply about connection: with people, with animals, with nature, and with the small, ordinary moments in a day. I try to live simply, gently, and with patience for myself and others, even when I don't always get it right.

I spent several years working in data and analytics consulting, primarily in marketing effectiveness and media measurement. It taught me a huge amount — how to build things properly, how to work with Python, how to turn messy problems into clean, usable systems, and how to communicate complex work clearly to people who need to act on it. But over time I realised the work itself, however well I did it, wasn't the thing I actually cared about. I was good at helping a company sell more. I want to spend my working life helping people (directly?) instead.

### What I've done

- **Consulting & analytics:** Several years building data pipelines, automating processes, and creating clear, well-structured systems for marketing effectiveness work — the kind of "get the infrastructure right so the real work can happen" role I consistently gravitated toward.
- **Python & lightweight building:** Comfortable writing and improving Python code, and more recently exploring AI-assisted ("vibecoded") tools — including an internal app that was showcased by the global CEO at Cannes Lions festival, an event described as the "Oscars" of advertising.
- **Supporting people directly:** Volunteering with Samaritans and acting as a mental health ally at work — listening to people in distress, without judgement, and holding space for them.
- **Teaching and sharing:** I really enjoy helping colleagues learn — running training sessions, unblocking people's technical problems, and pointing them toward tools and shortcuts that make their work easier.

### What I'm moving toward

I'm in the process of figuring out what to do next in my career. I'm still working out exactly what comes next, and I'm trying to hold that openly rather than rushing to a decision. What I do know is the direction: after years of work that rewarded precision and speed above all else, I want to do work that rewards patience, genuine listening, and care. Training as a Psychological Wellbeing Practitioner, supporting people with anxiety and depression through NHS Talking Therapies, is one path I'm seriously exploring — but I'm approaching it as one strong option among a few, not a fixed plan, and I'm giving myself the space to properly test that before committing.

### What I bring

- A rare mix of technical grounding (Python, data, systems thinking) and genuine interpersonal warmth — I've been told, and I believe, that people find me approachable and easy to talk to.
- Real experience sitting with people in distress, not just an interest in the idea of it.
- A generalist's mind: I'm not someone who wants to burrow narrowly into one technical niche forever — I'd rather understand a problem well enough to help, stay curious, and keep learning.
- A steady commitment to doing things properly rather than quickly — even when that's the harder, slower path.
- Persistent, practical altruism: I try to make choices, big and small, that reduce suffering and increase wellbeing, for people, for animals, and for the planet.

If any of this resonates — whether you're looking for support, want to talk about mental health, or just want to say hello — I'd love to hear from you.
